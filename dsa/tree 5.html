🌳 Deleting a Node from a Binary Search Tree

The Two Replacement Methods — In-order Successor & In-order Predecessor

📖 First: The 3 Cases of Deletion

Difficulty depends on how many children the node has:

🍃

Case 1: Leaf

No children → just remove it. Easy!

🪜

Case 2: One Child

Replace the node with its only child.

🌿

Case 3: Two Children ⭐

Can't just remove it — we replace its value using one of the two methods below!

⚖️ The Two Methods

Both keep the tree a valid BST — the in-order (sorted) sequence never changes.

🟢 Method 1 — In-order Successor

smallest node in the RIGHT subtree
  1. Go to the right subtree of the target
  2. Keep going LEFT until there is no left child
  3. Copy that value into the target, then delete it
🧭 How to find: One step RIGHT ➜ then all the way LEFT
DELETE 50 (successor = 60)

BEFORE                 AFTER
      50 ←delete            60 ←copied
     /  \                 /  \
   30    70             30    70
  /  \   / \           /  \     \
20   40 60  80       20   40    80
       ↑
   smallest in
   right subtree

🔵 Method 2 — In-order Predecessor

largest node in the LEFT subtree
  1. Go to the left subtree of the target
  2. Keep going RIGHT until there is no right child
  3. Copy that value into the target, then delete it
🧭 How to find: One step LEFT ➜ then all the way RIGHT
DELETE 50 (predecessor = 40)

BEFORE                 AFTER
      50 ←delete            40 ←copied
     /  \                 /  \
   30    70             30    70
  /  \   / \           /     /  \
20  40  60  80       20     60   80
    ↑
   largest in
   left subtree
💡 Memory trick: Successor = Right ➜ Left, left, left…  |  Predecessor = Left ➜ Right, right, right…
Why does the replacement always have ≤ 1 child? Because we walked all the way in one direction — there's nothing left on that side!
